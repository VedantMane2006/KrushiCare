# AgriSense Source Catalogue: Disease Advice & Agricultural Guidelines

This document records the official agricultural research sources supporting the diagnosis advice pack in AgriSense. In compliance with the project brief and team contracts, all guidance is drawn strictly from verified Indian agricultural institutes and government portals.

**Hierarchy of Sources:**
1. **ICAR Institutes:** ICAR-CPRI (Shimla), ICAR-IIHR (Bengaluru), ICAR-IIVR (Varanasi), ICAR-NCIPM (New Delhi)
2. **State Agricultural Universities (SAUs):** Tamil Nadu Agricultural University (TNAU) Agritech Portal
3. **Government Portals:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (Kisan Call Centre / Kisan Knowledge Management System, Krishi Vigyan Kendra Network)

**Date Checked:** 2026-10-30

## 1. Potato Disease & Management Sources

### 1.1 `potato__late_blight`
* **Common Name:** Potato Late Blight
* **Causal Agent:** *Phytophthora infestans* (Mont.) de Bary
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-Central Potato Research Institute (ICAR-CPRI) - Late Blight Management & Advisories
  * **URL:** https://cpri.icar.gov.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Potato (*Solanum tuberosum*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_potato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Rapidly expanding water-soaked purplish-brown/black lesions on leaves and stems; white cottony sporulation on lower leaf surface under high humidity (>90% RH) and cool temperatures (18–22°C).
  * Cultural management: Use certified disease-free seed tubers, ensure good field drainage, avoid overhead irrigation, cut haulms before harvest to prevent tuber contamination.
  * Chemical guidelines: Preventive contact fungicides (e.g. mancozeb) applied before canopy closure; systemic fungicide options upon early disease detection as advised by local extension officers.

### 1.2 `potato__early_blight`
* **Common Name:** Potato Early Blight
* **Causal Agent:** *Alternaria solani* Sorauer
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Potato (*Solanum tuberosum*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_potato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-CPRI Technical Bulletins on Potato Pathology
  * **URL:** https://cpri.icar.gov.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Brown-to-black angular/oval necrotic spots on older leaves exhibiting characteristic concentric rings ("bull's-eye" pattern).
  * Favorable conditions: Warm weather alternating with wet/dry cycles and high relative humidity.
  * Cultural management: Crop sanitation, removal and destruction of crop debris, 2–3 year crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops, balanced plant nutrition avoiding nitrogen stress.

### 1.3 `potato__healthy`
* **Common Name:** Healthy Potato Leaf / Preventive Crop Management
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-Central Potato Research Institute (ICAR-CPRI) - Potato Production Technology
  * **URL:** https://cpri.icar.gov.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Horticulture - Vegetables (Potato Cultivation Techniques)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/horti_vegetables_potato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Best practices for maintaining crop health: Sowing certified disease-free seed tubers, proper spacing, timely earthing up of soil to cover developing tubers, balanced NPK application, regular field scouting for early signs of foliar blight.

## 2. Tomato Disease & Pest Sources

### 2.1 `tomato__late_blight`
* **Common Name:** Tomato Late Blight
* **Causal Agent:** *Phytophthora infestans* (Mont.) de Bary
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Tomato (*Solanum lycopersici*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-Indian Institute of Horticultural Research (ICAR-IIHR) - Vegetable Pathology & Crop Protection
  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Water-soaked pale to dark brown lesions expanding rapidly across foliage; delicate white fungal growth on lower leaf surface under humid weather; green and ripening fruit developing dark, greasy lesions.
  * Cultural management: Removal and destruction of infected lower foliage, avoiding overhead sprinkler irrigation, staking plants to keep foliage off wet soil, crop rotation.

### 2.2 `tomato__early_blight`
* **Common Name:** Tomato Early Blight
* **Causal Agent:** *Alternaria solani*
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Tomato (*Solanum lycopersici*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-IIHR - Division of Plant Pathology (Epidemiology and Management of Foliar Diseases of Tomato)
  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Dark brown circular spots with concentric rings ("target" or "bull's-eye" rings) and yellow surrounding chlorosis starting on lower/older leaves; can cause defoliation and stem cankers.
  * Cultural management: Prune off lower diseased leaves, apply organic mulch to prevent soil rain splash, avoid handling foliage when wet, practice crop rotation.

### 2.3 `tomato__bacterial_spot`
* **Common Name:** Tomato Bacterial Leaf Spot
* **Causal Agent:** *Xanthomonas campestris* pv. *vesicatoria* (syn. *X. euvesicatoria* / *X. perforans*)
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Tomato (*Solanum lycopersici*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-IIHR - Characterization and Management of Bacterial Diseases in Solanaceous Vegetables
  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Small (2–3 mm), dark brown, water-soaked circular to angular spots with yellow halos on leaves; premature drop of severely spotted leaves; scabby, rough spots on green fruit.
  * Transmission & spread: Seed-borne pathogen; secondary spread by rain splashes and overhead sprinkler irrigation.
  * Cultural management: Certified disease-free seed, sanitation of seedling trays, avoiding overhead irrigation, not working in fields while plants are wet to prevent mechanical spread.

### 2.4 `tomato__septoria_leaf_spot`
* **Common Name:** Septoria Leaf Spot of Tomato
* **Causal Agent:** *Septoria lycopersici* Speg.
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Tomato (*Solanum lycopersici*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-Indian Institute of Vegetable Research (ICAR-IIVR) - Tomato Pathology
  * **URL:** https://iivr.icar.gov.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Numerous small, circular spots with grayish-white centers and distinctive dark brown borders; tiny black specks (pycnidia) visible inside mature spots; progress from bottom leaves upward causing heavy defoliation.
  * Cultural management: Removal and destruction of infected lower leaves, avoiding sprinkler irrigation, applying mulch, crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops.

### 2.5 `tomato__yellow_leaf_curl_virus`
* **Common Name:** Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV / ToLCV)
* **Causal Agent / Vector:** *Tomato leaf curl virus* (Begomovirus) transmitted by Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*)
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Tomato (*Solanum lycopersici*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-IIHR - Management of Whitefly and Vector-Borne Viruses in Tomato
  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Severe upward curling and crinkling of leaf margins, reduced leaf size, interveinal chlorosis (yellowing), shortened internodes giving plants a bushy, stunted appearance, few or no flowers/fruit.
  * Vector management: Raising seedlings under 40-mesh nylon nets, installing yellow sticky traps (5–10 per acre) to monitor and capture whiteflies, growing border barrier crops (maize, sorghum, or bajra), roguing and destroying early-infected plants.

### 2.6 `tomato__mosaic_virus`
* **Common Name:** Tomato Mosaic Virus (ToMV) / Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)
* **Causal Agent:** *Tomato mosaic virus* (Tobamovirus)
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Diseases of Tomato (*Solanum lycopersici*)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-IIHR - Plant Virology Advisory
  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Light and dark green mottled mosaic patterns on leaves, distortion or blistering of leaf surfaces, occasional "shoestring" or strap-like leaves, stunted plant growth.
  * Transmission & spread: Highly stable virus spread mechanically by hands, tools, staking equipment, contact between plants, and contaminated seed. Workers handling tobacco can transmit the virus.
  * Cultural management: Washing hands and tools thoroughly with soap and water or 10% trisodium phosphate before handling plants; strict roguing of infected plants; avoiding tobacco usage in the field; using certified virus-free seeds.

### 2.7 `tomato__spider_mites`
* **Common Name:** Two-Spotted Spider Mite / Red Spider Mite
* **Causal Agent:** *Tetranychus urticae* / *Tetranychus* spp. (Acari: Tetranychidae)
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Insect Pests of Tomato
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_insect_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR-IIHR - Division of Entomology & Nematology (Acarology)
  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Fine yellow speckling or stippling on the upper leaf surface caused by mites sucking sap from the underside; leaves turn bronzed or grayish-yellow; fine silken webbing on leaf undersides in heavy infestations; dry and warm weather accelerates outbreaks.
  * Cultural & biological management: Removing and destroying heavily infested leaves and field-border weeds; preserving predatory mites and beneficial coccinellids (*Stethorus* spp.); spraying clean water jets to disturb mite webs; applying recommended acaricides or sulfur under leaf canopies following local extension guidance.

### 2.8 `tomato__target_spot`
* **Common Name:** Target Spot of Tomato
* **Causal Agent:** *Corynespora cassiicola* (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) C.T. Wei
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal - Crop Protection Modules (Foliar Spots)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR Journal of Agricultural Sciences / Indian Phytopathology Reports on *Corynespora cassiicola*
  * **URL:** https://epubs.icar.org.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Small, pinpoint brown necrotic spots with chlorotic halos on foliage, enlarging into target-like concentric lesions; causes leaf blighting and defoliation under warm, humid conditions; sunken lesions on fruit.
  * Cultural management: Plant spacing to encourage air movement, avoiding overhead irrigation, prompt removal of crop debris post-harvest, crop rotation.
  * *Note on Gaps:* See Section 4 below regarding standalone extension literature in India.

### 2.9 `tomato__leaf_mold`
* **Common Name:** Tomato Leaf Mold
* **Causal Agent:** *Passalora fulva* (Cooke) U. Braun (syn. *Cladosporium fulvum* Cooke)
* **Primary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Crop Protection - Tomato Post-Harvest and Foliar Molds
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/crop_protection/crop_prot_crop_diseases_veg_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** ICAR - E-Course on Diseases of Horticultural Crops and Their Management
  * **URL:** https://ecourses.icar.gov.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Supported Information:**
  * Diagnostic symptoms: Pale greenish-yellow chlorotic spots with indistinct margins on upper leaf surfaces; velvety olive-green to grayish-brown fungal spore mold directly beneath the spots on the lower leaf surface; leaves turn yellow and drop.
  * Conditions: Favored by high relative humidity (>85%) and moderate temperatures; primarily encountered in protected cultivation, polyhouses, or dense sheltered canopies.
  * Cultural management: Improving ventilation, wide plant spacing, drip irrigation, sanitizing trellises and stakes.
  * *Note on Gaps:* See Section 4 below regarding prevalence in Indian open-field agriculture.

  * **URL:** https://iihr.res.in/
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30
* **Secondary Source:**
  * **Title:** TNAU Agritech Portal: Horticulture - Vegetables (Tomato Package of Practices)
  * **URL:** https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/horticulture/horti_vegetables_tomato.html
  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30

  * **Date Checked:** 2026-10-30

  * **Entity:** ICAR Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) Portal & Kisan Call Centre

## 4. Gaps and Observations Identified

During the audit and verification of official Indian agricultural sources, the following specific gaps were identified:

1. **`tomato__leaf_mold` (*Passalora fulva* / *Cladosporium fulvum*):**
   * *Status:* Documented in academic fungal lists and ICAR e-courses, but **lacks standalone active extension bulletin pages** on central ICAR extension portals.
   * *Reason:* Tomato leaf mold is predominantly an issue in greenhouse/polyhouse/high-tunnel systems with sustained relative humidity >85%. Because the overwhelming majority of smallholder tomato cultivation in India is open-field, extension priority is given to blights, wilts, and vector viruses.
   * *Remedy for Advice Pack:* Advice will focus on ventilation, canopy spacing, avoidance of overhead moisture, and consulting local KVK officers if grown under protected cultivation.

2. **`tomato__target_spot` (*Corynespora cassiicola*):**
   * *Status:* Reported in Indian phytopathology journals as an emerging pathogen on tomato and other tropical hosts, but rarely featured as a major standalone disease in primary state packages of practices.
   * *Reason:* In open-field Indian conditions, target spot symptoms closely mimic early blight (*Alternaria solani*) and bacterial spot, and are often managed concurrently with standard broad-spectrum foliar protective schedules.
   * *Remedy for Advice Pack:* Guidance will emphasize field sanitation, avoiding leaf wetness, and crop rotation, harmonized with TNAU's general foliar spot advisories.

3. **Pesticide Dose Discipline:**
   * In strict accordance with user guidelines and safety rules, specific chemical milligram/gram dosages are omitted. Advice will state the recognized chemical class or active ingredient as catalogued in the TNAU/ICAR sources, accompanied by the mandatory instruction: *"Always follow the label dose and ask your local agriculture officer"*.
